## What it says

1. **Unsaturated, the proposed APF is clearly better than B1** where the paper says it
   should be: GNRON (S2: 83 % vs 0 %), symmetric trap (S3-line: 100 % vs 83 %), corridor
   (S4: 50 % vs 0 %), swap (S5: 100 % vs 0 %), and it never collided in S1–S4.
2. **With a_max = 5 m/s² almost everything collides, for both methods.** Cause: the
   static-slot quadratic attraction starts at k_a·L ≈ 20–30 m/s² and accelerates the
   swarm to ~9–10 m/s; at that speed 5 m/s² of braking is not enough near obstacles.
   This is the D006 / Proposition 2 issue at full size, not a bug.
3. **S5 two groups, unsaturated: the APF run blew up numerically** (speeds 1e14–1e26
   m/s). Closing speed ~20 m/s crosses the 0.7 m barrier band in ~3 RK4 steps of
   10 ms; the stiff barrier is not resolved. The continuous-time guarantee does not
   transfer to RK4 at dt = 0.01 s at that speed.
4. **S1 forest, unsaturated: APF never collides but never finishes in 60 s** (final
   formation error several metres). Probably agents parked behind trees (local minima
   the reset does not resolve) or Morse coupling holding them back; needs a look.
5. **S3-U (concave)**: nobody succeeds; APF at least does not collide.
6. B1 "collisions" are mostly separations just below d_min = 0.5 m (B1's pair
   repulsion is singular at r = 0, not at d_min). Fairness of that baseline choice
   should be decided before T17.

## Decisions needed (proposals)

- **Q1 — speed of approach (most important).** Options: (a) move the formation centre
  along a reference path at a bounded speed (main.tex Remark "tracking"; the controller
  needs the feed-forward term ṗ*); (b) shorten L; (c) add a speed limit v_max ≈ 2–3 m/s.
  Proposal: (a) for the paper's main experiments, because it is what a real formation
  does and it makes a_max = 5 m/s² realistic; keep the static case for the theory check.
- **Q2 — integrator.** With (a) speeds drop and dt = 0.01 s should suffice; otherwise
  use dt = 0.002 s or adaptive RK45 near the barrier (D002 allows revisiting).
- **Q3 — B1 pair repulsion:** keep Khatib on r (classic) or shift to r − d_min (fairer).
- **Q4 — S1 stalls:** inspect a stalled S1 run (`scripts/run_scenario.py` on a saved
  scenario) once Q1 is settled.

Max speeds now 1.5–6.7 m/s (was ~10 m/s). With the reference moving, the saturated
(a_max = 5) and unsaturated results are almost identical, i.e. Proposition 2's condition
is effectively met.

Without D010 (T1 factor on the moving slot) the same prototype gave, on S1–S4 N = 5:
8 % success and 92 % collision or numerical blow-up; per-run inspection showed agents
riding their slot straight through an obstacle (g ≈ 0 switches T1 off) and blow-ups
(speeds up to 1e27 m/s) when an agent was held back while its slot moved on.

Remaining weak spots: **S1 forest** (agents stuck behind trees, 10–15 resets per run, final
errors 15–20 m) and **S3-U concave trap** (0 % for both methods). These are the
deficiency D2 cases; T3 + reset do not yet solve them at these gains (tuning T17, or a
stronger escape rule, to discuss).
